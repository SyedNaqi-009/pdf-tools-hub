In almost every modern office, two file formats dominate daily communication: Microsoft Word (.docx) and Adobe Portable Document Format (.pdf). While both formats store text, images, and tables, they serve fundamentally different purposes in document lifecycles.

Using the wrong format at the wrong stage can lead to formatting errors, accidental overwrites, or embarrassing layout shifts. Here is a definitive guide on when to use each format and how to transition smoothly between them.

## When You Should Always Use Word (.docx)

1. **Active Drafting & Brainstorming:** When team members need to comment, suggest edits, and revise wording in real time.
2. **Template Authoring:** Creating standardized letterheads or report skeletons that colleagues will fill in later.
3. **Dynamic Data Merges:** Generating mass personalized letters using mail merge features.

## When You Should Always Use PDF (.pdf)

1. **Formal Contracts & Agreements:** Once terms are finalized, lock them into a PDF and sign them using our [Sign PDF Tool](/tools/sign-pdf).
2. **Job Resumes & CVs:** Sending a resume in Word format risks having your careful margins shifted by the recruiter software version. A PDF guarantees your formatting remains pristine.
3. **Invoices & Quotes:** Customers should never be able to accidentally edit line items or pricing on billing documents.
4. **Official Archiving:** PDFs comply with ISO 19005 (PDF/A) archiving standards, guaranteeing readability decades into the future.
